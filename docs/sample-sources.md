# Sample sources and licensing

The software works with audio you provide. A source being free to download does
not necessarily mean it may be repackaged inside an MPC instrument, and a
license that permits released music may still forbid sample redistribution.

## Good source categories

### Your own recordings and synthesis

This is the clearest option for redistributable examples. Record, synthesize,
or mathematically generate the source, keep session notes, and choose an
explicit license when publishing it. The portable demo follows this model.

### Samples From Mars

[Samples From Mars collections](https://samplesfrommars.com/collections) are a
strong local source for classic drum machines and synthesizers. Products are
organized as WAV files and many include MPC formats. The company's
[FAQ](https://samplesfrommars.com/pages/faq) allows royalty-free use in music
but prohibits transferring, repackaging, or redistributing its products and
sounds.

Use these packs to build programs for yourself. Do not commit their WAVs,
generated XPMs containing copied audio, or redistributable demo bundles. This
project may publish conversion recipes, filename rules, audits, and listening
notes that contain no sample audio.

### CC0 and attribution-licensed libraries

The [TidalCycles TR-808 Fischer collection](https://github.com/tidalcycles/sounds-tr808-fischer)
publishes 116 TR-808 samples under CC0 1.0 and is useful for openly
redistributable Drum Program fixtures.

[Freesound](https://freesound.org/help/faq/) has CC0, CC BY, and CC BY-NC
uploads. Filter per sound, retain its page URL and license, provide attribution
for CC BY, and keep noncommercial material out of general-purpose published
instruments. Because uploads are user-supplied, also assess provenance instead
of relying only on the displayed license.

### Free for music, not for repackaging

[MusicRadar SampleRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/tech/free-music-samples-royalty-free-loops-hits-and-multis-to-download-sampleradar)
samples are royalty-free for music but may not be redistributed as sample
collections. Treat them like commercial packs: local builds are useful,
published MPC bundles are not.

[Pianobook's terms](https://www.pianobook.co.uk/terms-conditions/) allow use in
compositions but prohibit redistribution and reformatting as sampler
instruments. Do not publish converted Pianobook MPC programs.

[Philharmonia's resources](https://philharmonia.co.uk/resources/) provide
downloadable orchestral samples, but verify the terms linked to the specific
resource and intended redistribution before building anything public.

## Provenance record

For every source, record:

- product or collection name;
- creator and canonical URL;
- exact per-file or collection license;
- download date and version, when available;
- source archive or WAV checksum;
- attribution text, when required;
- whether local conversion and redistribution are permitted separately; and
- transformations applied by the build.

When terms are unclear, default to local personal use and publish only the
recipe or metadata. A source manifest is evidence, not legal advice, and does
not override the source owner's terms.
